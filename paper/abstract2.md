Fingerprint datasets such as SOCOFing are widely used for biometric recognition research but are not designed for small-object detection scenarios involving printed fingerprints embedded in larger document images. Practical document-analysis applications require the detection of small fingerprints captured under real acquisition conditions, including low contrast, non-uniform illumination, noise, and partial occlusions.

This work presents a reproducible methodology for transforming a recognition-oriented fingerprint dataset into a small-object detection dataset suitable for document processing applications. Starting from SOCOFing, fingerprint regions were automatically localized and converted into YOLO-compatible annotations. A synthetic dataset generation pipeline was subsequently developed to emulate realistic deployment conditions by introducing random spatial placement, multiple object scales, partial occlusions, and acquisition noise.

A YOLOv8n detector trained on the resulting synthetic dataset achieved a precision of 0.989, recall of 0.997, mAP50 of 0.995, and mAP50-95 of 0.995 during validation. Despite these excellent metrics, early real-world tests revealed significant performance degradation when processing captured document images. A systematic diagnostic process identified image contrast as the dominant limiting factor. Experiments demonstrated that histogram equalization and Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) substantially improved fingerprint detectability without any additional training.

The results highlight the importance of acquisition conditions and preprocessing pipelines in practical deployments, showing that improvements in image quality may provide larger gains than additional training once a detector reaches high validation performance. The proposed workflow offers a reproducible framework for adapting biometric datasets to small-object detection tasks and for analyzing the gap between synthetic training environments and real-world document acquisition.
